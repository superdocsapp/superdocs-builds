## C-1. The granting organization

The Continental Society for Energy Storage Engineering (CSESE) is a professional and learned
society incorporated in 1974, with its secretariat at 300 Marchmont Row, Halifax, Nova Scotia.
Its stated purpose is the advancement of engineering practice in electrical and electrochemical
energy storage.

Extracted from the Society's 2024 Annual Report, filed with its members and published on its
public register:

- **Individual members:** 41,880, resident in 63 countries.
- **Corporate and institutional members:** 1,214, including 38 utilities, 22 national laboratories
  and 61 universities.
- **Regional chapters:** 29, across North America, Central and South America, and affiliated
  chapters in Europe and East Asia.
- **Publications:** the Society publishes three peer-reviewed journals and the *CSESE Storage
  Practice Code*, referenced by four state public utility commissions.
- **Annual congress:** the 2024 congress in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, registered 6,340 attendees
  from 41 countries.

The Society is not affiliated with, funded by, or otherwise connected to Voltaris Grid Dynamics,
Inc., the Kessendra Institute of Applied Energetics, or Dr. Ferreira-Duarte.

## C-2. The award, its bylaws and its selection criteria

The Halvorsen Medal was endowed in 1997 by bequest of Ingrid Halvorsen-Baak and has been conferred
annually since 1998. The following is reproduced from Article 9 of the CSESE Bylaws, revised
edition of March 2023, which is published in full on the Society's public register.

> **9.1 Purpose.** The Halvorsen Medal in Energy Storage Engineering shall be conferred annually
> upon one individual whose published or applied engineering work has, in the judgement of the
> Medal Panel, materially advanced the safety, reliability, or efficiency of stored electrical
> energy systems.
>
> **9.2 Eligibility.** Any engineer or scientist working in the field is eligible without regard
> to nationality, place of residence, employer, or membership in the Society. Self-nomination is
> not permitted.
>
> **9.3 Criteria of assessment.** The Medal Panel shall assess candidates solely on the following,
> in the order stated: (a) the originality of the candidate's technical contribution; (b) the
> demonstrated adoption of that contribution by parties independent of the candidate; (c) the
> measurable improvement in safety, reliability, or efficiency attributable to it; and (d) the
> durability of the contribution over not fewer than three years. Standing, seniority, employer,
> and institutional affiliation shall not be considered.
>
> **9.4 The Medal Panel.** The Panel shall consist of seven members: the immediate past President
> of the Society, four members appointed by the Council from among persons holding senior academic
> or engineering appointments in the field, and two members nominated by the Society's affiliated
> national bodies. No panelist may serve who is employed by, has co-authored with, or has a
> financial interest in any organization employing a candidate under consideration.
>
> **9.5 Procedure.** Nominations close on 31 January. The Panel shall reduce nominations to a
> shortlist of not more than six, each of whom shall be assessed against Article 9.3 in a written
> report. The Medal shall be conferred by not fewer than five of the seven votes. Where no
> candidate attains five votes, the Medal shall not be conferred that year. The Medal has been
> withheld in 2003, 2009 and 2017.
