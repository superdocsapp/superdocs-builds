## 1. What we do

Calderon Ridge designs and commissions industrial motion-control and machine-vision systems. ## 3. Why the position requires a degree in a specific specialty

Each duty above rests on university-level engineering theory that cannot be acquired on the job. Sizing a servo axis correctly requires rigid-body dynamics and rotational inertia analysis. Tuning a closed loop without destroying a mechanism requires control theory — pole placement, gain and phase margin, and sampling effects in discrete-time systems. Calculating a performance level under ISO 13849-1 requires reliability engineering and probability. Calibrating a camera frame to a robot frame requires linear algebra and homogeneous transformations. An engineer who has not studied these subjects formally cannot do this work, and a mistake in any of them is a safety event on a customer's production floor, not a defect ticket.

For that reason Calderon Ridge requires, as a condition of employment and without exception, a bachelor's degree or higher in Mechatronics Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Control Systems Engineering, Robotics Engineering, or a closely related engineering specialty for every Controls Systems Engineer position. Our written engineering hiring standard, which states this requirement, is enclosed at Tab C. We have filled this role six times since 2018 and every person hired held a degree in one of those specialties; their credentials are listed in the hiring standard document.

The Engineer II level is distinct from our Engineer I level, which is limited to supervised implementation of an architecture someone else designed, and from our Automation Technician role, which requires an associate degree and performs wiring, panel build, and guided fault-finding under an engineer's direction. Only at the Engineer II level does an engineer own the control architecture and sign the safety validation report. Our organizational chart at Tab F shows this structure.
