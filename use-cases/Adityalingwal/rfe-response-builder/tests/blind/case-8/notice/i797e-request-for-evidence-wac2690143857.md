### REQUEST 1 — THE PROFFERED POSITION AS A SPECIALTY OCCUPATION

**Governing standard.** To qualify as a specialty occupation, the offered position must require (a) theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge, and (b) attainment of a bachelor's or higher degree in the specific specialty, or its equivalent, as a minimum for entry into the occupation in the United States. INA § 214(i)(1); 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(ii). The position must further satisfy at least one criterion at 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(A)(*1*)–(*4*).

**Determination.** The record identifies the offered position by title and general occupational category but does not, on the evidence presently submitted, establish that the specific duties to be performed are so specialized and complex, or the body of knowledge required so particularized, that attainment of a bachelor's degree **in a specific specialty** is normally the minimum requirement for entry. A degree requirement stated in general terms, or one satisfied by a range of unrelated fields, does not by itself establish a specialty occupation.

**Submit any or all of the following:**

1.1 A detailed written description of the offered position, setting out each duty the beneficiary will actually perform, the approximate percentage of time allocated to each duty, and the specific body of specialized knowledge each duty requires.

1.2 A statement identifying the specific academic specialty or specialties that are the minimum requirement for entry into the position, and an explanation of how each named specialty is directly related to the duties described in 1.1.

1.3 Evidence of the petitioner's normal hiring practice for this and parallel positions — for example, prior job announcements, position descriptions maintained in the ordinary course of business, or an organizational chart identifying the position within the petitioner's structure and its supervisory and reporting relationships.

1.4 Any independent evidence you wish USCIS to consider on industry practice among similar organizations in parallel positions, including but not limited to authoritative occupational references or an opinion from a person qualified to render one.

### REQUEST 3 — EMPLOYER-EMPLOYEE RELATIONSHIP AND AVAILABILITY OF SPECIALTY OCCUPATION WORK FOR THE ENTIRE PERIOD REQUESTED

**Governing standard.** The petitioner must establish that it is a United States employer, that it will have and will maintain an employer-employee relationship with the beneficiary throughout the requested validity period, and that specialty occupation work will in fact be available to the beneficiary for that entire period. 8 C.F.R. §§ 214.2(h)(2)(i)(B), 214.2(h)(4)(ii). Where the beneficiary will be placed at one or more third-party worksites, the petitioner must establish the actual work to be performed at each such location and its own continuing right to control the beneficiary's work. An itinerary of definite employment is required where services will be performed in more than one location. 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(h)(2)(i)(B).

**Determination.** The record indicates that the beneficiary's services are to be provided in connection with an arrangement involving one or more parties other than the petitioner. Contractual documentation that establishes only a framework for future engagements, without evidence of the specific work actually assigned and the period it covers, does not establish that non-speculative specialty occupation work exists for the full validity period requested. Speculative employment is not a basis for approval.

**Submit any or all of the following:**

3.1 Signed work orders, statements of work, purchase orders, or task orders in effect for the requested validity period, identifying the beneficiary by name or by the specific role to be filled, the services to be performed, the location of performance, and the term.

3.3 A complete itinerary of services for the entire requested validity period of 06/15/2026 through 06/14/2029, giving the dates and locations of each assignment. 3.4 Evidence of the petitioner's retained right to control the beneficiary's work, which may include the applicable provisions of the governing agreement, the beneficiary's reporting line, the petitioner's performance review and timekeeping practices as applied to the beneficiary, and evidence of the petitioner's authority to reassign or remove the beneficiary from an assignment.
