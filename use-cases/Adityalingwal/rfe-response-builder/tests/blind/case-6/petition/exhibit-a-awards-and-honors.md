## A-1. Thornfield Prize for Composition in New Media — Recipient, 2023

**Certificate text (reproduced from the original, Tab A-1a):**

> The Thornfield Foundation for the Sonic Arts confers upon **Mireille
> Sato-Adeyemi** the Thornfield Prize for Composition in New Media, for the score
> and sound design of *Tidewater Requiem*. Conferred at Rockvale, Illinois, on
> the eleventh day of November, two thousand twenty-three.
> Signed: Perpetua Anselm-Dray, Chair of the Board of Trustees.

**Granting body.** The Thornfield Foundation for the Sonic Arts was established
in 1988 and is headquartered in Rockvale, Illinois. It administers three annual
prizes in composition and sound design and maintains a permanent archive of
prize-winning works. The Foundation's prizes are announced each November and
have been reported on annually since 1994 by *The Ardenmoor Review*, *Sonics
Quarterly* and the *Cascadia Culture Ledger* (see Exhibit C for the 2023 items).

**Scope and eligibility (Tab A-1b, Foundation rules, 2023 edition, §2).** The
prize is open to composers and sound designers of any nationality, resident in
any country, whose work premiered anywhere in the world during the two calendar
years preceding the award. There is no age limit and no residency requirement.
Self-nomination is not permitted; candidates must be put forward by a presenting
organization, a conservatory, or a previous laureate.

**Selection process (Tab A-1b, §4–§6).** Nominations are screened by the
Foundation secretariat for eligibility only, then scored blind by a five-member
jury against four published criteria: originality of sonic language, integration
with the staged work, technical execution, and influence on practice in the
field. The jury's shortlist is published; the final vote requires a simple
majority.

**Candidate pool, 2023 cycle (Tab A-1c, Foundation announcement of 11 November
2023).** 214 eligible nominations were received from 31 countries. 12 works were
shortlisted. One prize was conferred.

## A-2. Golden Compass Award, Nordlys Elektronika Festival — Recipient, 2022

**Citation (Tab A-2a):** Awarded to Mireille Sato-Adeyemi for the live work
*Salt Tide Variations*, presented on the Main Stage, Vestervik, Denmark, on
19 August 2022.

**Granting body and scope (Tab A-2b, festival regulations, art. 3).** Nordlys
Elektronika Festival has been held annually in Vestervik, Denmark since 1997. The
Golden Compass Award is decided among all artists programmed on the festival's
Main Stage in a given edition; the 2022 Main Stage programme comprised 38 artists
from 19 countries, selected from open submissions and curatorial invitation. The
award is international in scope in that eligibility is not restricted by
nationality or country of residence.

**Selection panel, 2022 edition (Tab A-2b):** a three-member panel of the
festival's Artistic Director (Jóhanna Steinsdóttir-Bregg), the Director of the
Vestervik Municipal Concert House (Aksel Rombauer-Thys), and one invited critic
(Marisol Quintero-Ffrench, *Hollowdrum*). Panel members are named in the
published festival programme each year.

**Independent reporting (Tab A-2c):** the 2022 award announcement was covered by
*Sonics Quarterly* and the *Cascadia Culture Ledger*; copies of both items are at
Exhibit C, rows C-2 and C-3.

## A-3. Sable Coast Emerging Voice Award — Nominee (shortlisted), 2021

**Nomination notice (Tab A-3a), dated 4 June 2021.** Ms. Sato-Adeyemi was one of
six nominees shortlisted from 88 submissions for the Sable Coast Emerging Voice
Award, conferred annually by the Sable Coast Music Festival, Ardvale Harbour,
Nova Scotia. Eligibility under the festival's published rules (Tab A-3b, §1) is
limited to artists who are citizens or residents of Canada and who have not
previously released a full-length commercial recording. **The petitioner notes
that this award is national in scope and is relied upon as corroborative only.**

**2021 selection panel (Tab A-3b):** Ines Achterberg-Ruiz (Artistic Director,
Sable Coast Music Festival); Théo Nkemdirim-Lassalle (programme director,
Ardvale Harbour Arts Council); Priya Sundaralingam-Beck (composer, 2018
recipient).
