### 1. Managerial capacity of the U.S. position — FULLY COVERED

Every sub-part is answered: the duty breakdown with percentages appears in both `02-us-employer-support-letter.md` (§3) and `06-position-description.md` (§2); the four direct reports are named with titles, degrees, and duty summaries and their own headcounts in `02` (§4), `04-organizational-charts.md` (Chart 1 detail table), and `06` (§3); hiring, firing, promotion, and compensation authority is spelled out in `02` (§§3–5) and `06` (duty C); and the U.S. organizational chart naming the positions above and below the beneficiary is `04`, Chart 1.

### 2. Qualifying relationship — FULLY COVERED

`05-corporate-and-business-records.md` supplies each item the officer names: the certified Delaware certificate of incorporation with no amendments (E-1), the sole issued stock certificate for all 1,000 shares (E-2), the complete stock transfer ledger (E-3), the board minutes authorizing the issuance (E-4), and proof the consideration was actually paid — the Banco Meridional wire confirmation plus the matching Cardinal Ridge Bank statement (E-5), reinforced by the 2025 sole-shareholder consent showing continuing control (E-6).

### 3. One continuous year of qualifying employment abroad — PARTIALLY COVERED

The claim itself is well described — `03-foreign-entity-support-letter.md` (§§2–3) gives the title, the unbroken dates from February 1, 2021, the duty percentages, the subordinate managers, and the personnel and budget authority, and `04` Chart 2 shows the position in the Brazilian structure — but none of the three corroborating items the officer asks for is in the record: no pay slips, wage statements, or Brazilian tax withholding records (a); no employment contract, appointment letter, or personnel file extract (b); and no I-94s, no passport entry/exit stamp pages, and no month-by-month presence statement (c), only a passport biographic page listed at Exhibit G in `01`. The officer's May–November 2023 U.S. admission is also left unreconciled: `02` (§6) refers to the beneficiary leading "the Austin transition workshop series in 2023" while `03` asserts uninterrupted service in Brazil, and nothing in the petition explains the two together.

### 4. Both entities doing business — FULLY COVERED

`05` documents regular, systematic business on both sides: for the petitioner, three years of reviewed financial statements rising to USD 6.42 million in FY2024 (E-8), the Form 1120 and all four 2024 Forms 941 (E-9), and three executed master services agreements plus a 47-invoice schedule for 2024 (E-10); for the foreign employer, audited 2023 and 2024 statements (E-11) and four executed client contracts with a reconciling invoice schedule (E-12).

### 5. U.S. physical premises — NOT COVERED

No petition document contains or even references a lease, deed, floor plan, square-footage statement, or photograph of any location. The Austin address appears only on the petitioner's letterhead in `02` and in the general statement that staff are "based at our Austin office"; an address on a letterhead is not evidence that the petitioner occupies premises, and the corporate exhibit index in `05` runs from E-1 to E-12 without any premises tab.

### 6. Corroborating personnel evidence for the subordinates — PARTIALLY COVERED

The staffing structure is documented and one aggregate payroll figure exists — `05` (E-9) reports 14 employees and USD 1,082,400 in wages on the Q4 2024 Form 941, and `02` (§4), `04`, and `06` (§3) name each direct report with title, degree, institution, and year — but nothing individual is supplied: no W-2 or pay statements for any of the four managers, no diploma, transcript, or credential evaluation for any of them, and no occupational evidence that a bachelor's degree is the normal minimum for entry into these roles beyond the petitioner's own assertion in `06` (§3).

## Notes for test authors

- The deliberate internal tension is between `02` (§6) and `03` (§2): a 2023 Austin workshop series versus continuous employment in Brazil. It is relevant to request 3 and is never resolved in the petition.
- Request 5 is the clean "no evidence anywhere" case; a system that treats the letterhead address as premises evidence has produced a false positive.
- Requests 3 and 6 both fail on corroboration rather than on assertion — the facts are stated, the documents proving them are absent.
