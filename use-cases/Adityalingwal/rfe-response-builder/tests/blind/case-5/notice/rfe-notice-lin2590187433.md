## WHY WE SENT YOU THIS NOTICE

We reviewed the Form I-140 petition you filed and the evidence submitted with it. We
need additional evidence before we can finish processing your case. This notice tells
you what evidence we need and why the evidence already in the record is not enough.

This notice does not decide your case. It is not a denial. Sending additional evidence
does not guarantee approval.

To establish eligibility for a national interest waiver, the record must first show
that the beneficiary qualifies as a member of the professions holding an advanced
degree or as an individual of exceptional ability under 8 CFR 204.5(k). If that
threshold is met, the record must then satisfy the three-part framework set out in
*Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016):

- **Prong 1** — the proposed endeavor has both substantial merit and national importance;
- **Prong 2** — the beneficiary is well positioned to advance the proposed endeavor; and
- **Prong 3** — on balance, it would be beneficial to the United States to waive the
  job offer and labor certification requirements.

The petitioner bears the burden of proof by a preponderance of the evidence.
INA § 291; *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369 (AAO 2010).

### ITEM 1. THE PROPOSED ENDEAVOR

**What the record shows.** You submitted a narrative describing an endeavor involving
selective lithium recovery from geothermal brine using layered-sorbent membrane modules,
together with a statement of intended activities in the United States.

**What we need.** Confirm and, if necessary, supplement the description of the specific
endeavor the beneficiary proposes to undertake. Your response must identify:

1.a. the precise nature of the work, stated with specificity rather than as a general
     field of research or a general occupation;

1.b. where and through what entity or entities the work will be carried out; and

1.c. the prospective impact of the endeavor, described in terms broader than the
     beneficiary's own employment or the interests of a single employer.

### ITEM 2. QUALIFYING CLASSIFICATION — ADVANCED DEGREE

**What the record shows.** The record contains a curriculum vitae in which the
beneficiary states that she holds a Doctor of Philosophy in Chemical Engineering awarded
in 2019 by Väinamere Technical University, Tartu, Estonia, and a Master of Science
awarded by the same institution in 2015. A self-prepared curriculum vitae is not primary
evidence of an academic credential.

**Why this is not sufficient.** Under 8 CFR 204.5(k)(3)(i)(A), a petition for an
advanced degree professional must be accompanied by an official academic record showing
that the beneficiary has a United States advanced degree or a foreign equivalent degree.
The record before us contains no official academic record and no evaluation of the
foreign credential.

**What we need.** Submit **all** of the following:

2.a. a copy of the official diploma or degree certificate for each degree claimed;

2.b. official transcripts issued by the awarding institution, listing courses completed,
     credits, and the date the degree was conferred;

2.c. a credential evaluation from a recognized credential evaluation service
     establishing that each foreign degree claimed is equivalent to a degree awarded by
     an accredited United States college or university; and

2.d. certified English translations, together with the translator's certificate of
     competence, for any document not in English, as required by 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3).

### ITEM 3. WELL POSITIONED TO ADVANCE THE ENDEAVOR

**What the record shows.** You submitted a curriculum vitae, a statement of the proposed
endeavor, and letters from three individuals.

**What we need.** Submit evidence addressing the beneficiary's education, skills,

3.d. a plan describing the activities the beneficiary intends to carry out, the resources

### ITEM 4. IMPACT OF THE BENEFICIARY'S PUBLISHED WORK

**What the record shows.** The record refers to the beneficiary's publications and to
their reception in the field.

**What we need.** Submit objective documentary evidence, from sources independent of the

4.b. the relevant excerpts of citing works, so that the nature of the reliance placed on

4.c. evidence that a method, protocol, standard, or design attributed to the beneficiary

### ITEM 5. INTEREST OF INVESTORS, CUSTOMERS, USERS, OR GOVERNMENT ENTITIES

**What the record shows.** The record contains correspondence indicating preliminary
interest by at least one entity in the beneficiary's technology.

**Why this is not sufficient.** Expressions of interest that are conditional,
non-binding, or contingent on future events establish that an entity is aware of the
beneficiary's work. They do not, without more, establish that relevant parties have
committed resources to it.

**What we need.** Submit evidence of concrete, realized interest, such as:

5.a. executed contracts, purchase orders, subscription agreements, or service agreements
     naming the beneficiary or her organization as a party, signed and dated by all
     parties;

5.b. evidence of funding actually received — investment agreements, wire confirmations,
     capitalization tables, executed grant or award notices, or contract award notices —
     rather than applications, proposals, or submissions still pending decision;

5.c. financial records showing revenue attributable to the endeavor, such as invoices
     issued and paid, or federal or state business tax returns; and

5.d. if the endeavor is carried on through a United States business entity, evidence of
     that entity's existence and operation, including its articles of incorporation or
     organization, and evidence of the beneficiary's ownership interest and role.

### ITEM 6. EXPERT OPINION LETTERS

**What the record shows.** You submitted letters from three individuals commenting on
the beneficiary's work. Two of the authors identify themselves as the beneficiary's
former doctoral supervisor and as a co-author of her publications. The third author
identifies himself as an official of an entity that has itself corresponded with the
beneficiary regarding a prospective deployment.

**Why this is not sufficient.** Letters from individuals who have supervised,
collaborated with, or have a prospective business relationship with the beneficiary are
given limited weight standing alone, because the authors are not independent of the
beneficiary. Letters that recite conclusions without describing the underlying facts are
likewise of limited probative value. *Matter of Caron International*, 19 I&N Dec. 791
(Comm'r 1988).

**What we need.** Submit:

6.a. letters from experts in the field who have **not** supervised the beneficiary, have
     **not** co-authored or collaborated with her, and have **no** employment, ownership,
     investment, or prospective commercial relationship with her or her organization;

6.b. for **each** letter in the record — including those already submitted — the author's
     curriculum vitae or a statement of the author's own credentials in the field;

6.c. for each such letter, a statement explaining how the author became aware of the
     beneficiary's work and on what specific documents or results the author's opinion
     rests; and

6.d. a written disclosure of any professional, financial, academic, or familial
     relationship between each author and the beneficiary.

### ITEM 8. BALANCING — BENEFIT OF WAIVING THE JOB OFFER REQUIREMENT

**What the record shows.** You submitted argument on the third prong of *Matter of
Dhanasar*.

**What we need.** Submit evidence supporting the assertion that, on balance, it would be
beneficial to the United States to waive the requirements of a job offer and thus of a
labor certification. Relevant evidence includes:

8.a. an explanation of why it would be impractical for the beneficiary to secure a job
     offer or for a United States employer to obtain a labor certification on her behalf;

8.b. a statement of the beneficiary's employment status and of whether the endeavor is

8.c. evidence of urgency, if urgency is claimed, including any timetable, program
     deadline, or supply constraint that the endeavor is intended to address; and

8.d. an explanation of the benefit the United States would derive from the beneficiary's
