## 2. Legal standard

Section 203(b)(2)(B)(i) of the Act permits the Attorney General to waive the job offer
requirement, and therefore the labor certification, for a member of the professions
holding an advanced degree when the waiver is in the national interest. The framework is
set out in *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016). The standard of proof is
preponderance of the evidence. *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010).

## 3. Prong one — substantial merit and national importance

The proposed endeavor is described in full at Exhibit A. In short, Dr. Brasch will
continue to develop, field-test, and place into commercial operation a direct lithium
extraction module that recovers battery-grade lithium from geothermal brine without
evaporation ponds and without shipping brine off site.

The merit of the endeavor is technical and economic. Lithium is a designated critical
mineral, and the overwhelming share of refined supply is produced outside the United
States. Domestic geothermal brine in the Great Basin carries lithium at concentrations
that conventional evaporation cannot economically address, and evaporation is in any
event unavailable at the water budgets prevailing in northern Nevada. The endeavor is
directed at a supply constraint that reaches beyond any single firm: it affects domestic
cell manufacturing, grid storage procurement, and the operators of geothermal plants that
today reinject a saleable mineral back into the ground.

The endeavor's importance is national rather than local. The process is not tied to one
resource area; it applies to brines across the Basin and Range and to oilfield produced
water in other states, as Exhibit A explains at section 4.

## 4. Prong two — well positioned to advance the endeavor

Dr. Brasch holds a doctorate in chemical engineering and has worked on selective ion
capture for eleven years (Exhibit B). She is the sole first author of the sorbent
chemistry underlying the module, and her published work has been taken up by researchers

She has moved beyond publication into working hardware. Exhibit A, section 5, records the

have drawn from a regional water authority, a geothermal operator, and a state innovation
fund.

## 5. Prong three — on balance, the waiver benefits the United States

**No employer exists to sponsor her, and none realistically could.** Dr. Brasch is a
self-petitioner. She is the founder and Chief Technology Officer of Halide Loop
Technologies, Inc., the company through which the endeavor is carried out, and she holds
a controlling interest in it. A labor certification tests whether a United States worker
is available for a position offered by an employer. There is no employer here other than
the company Dr. Brasch herself created, and requiring her to obtain an offer from a third
party would require her to abandon the endeavor in order to qualify to pursue it.

**The labor certification test does not measure what matters here.** The question the
certification process answers — whether a qualified United States worker is available for
a defined position — is not the question this endeavor turns on. What the endeavor
depends on is the specific sorbent chemistry Dr. Brasch developed and the operating
knowledge accumulated across four years of runs on real brine. A comparably credentialed
engineer hired into a defined position would not carry that work forward; the work would
begin again.

**Delay carries a concrete cost.** The Nevada Division of Minerals has set the closing
date for the second round of geothermal brine mineral lease applications at March 31,
2027 (Exhibit A, section 6). An applicant must demonstrate an operating recovery method
at the time of application. If Dr. Brasch cannot direct the field program through 2026,
the lease round passes and the demonstration site is lost for the term of the leases
awarded.

**The benefit does not depend on scarcity.** Even accepting that other qualified chemical
engineers are available in the United States, the benefit at issue is not the filling of
a vacancy. It is the continuation of a specific line of work that has already produced a
functioning module and that responds to a documented national supply problem.
