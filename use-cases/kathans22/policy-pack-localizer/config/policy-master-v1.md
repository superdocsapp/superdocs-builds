## 1 Purpose and Commitment

Meridian Relief Trust (MRT) exists to protect and assist people affected by crisis, and it holds an unequivocal commitment to safeguard the people it serves, its staff, and its partners from harm caused by those acting in its name. This policy sets out the standards of conduct expected of everyone connected with MRT — employees, volunteers, consultants, board members, and implementing partners — regardless of role, seniority, or location. Safeguarding failures cause profound harm to survivors and undermine the trust communities place in humanitarian actors; MRT treats every safeguarding concern with the seriousness it deserves. This policy is grounded in the do-no-harm principle and in MRT's belief that beneficiaries have an absolute right to be free from exploitation, abuse, and harassment by anyone associated with the organisation. Leadership at every level is accountable for embedding this policy into recruitment, induction, training, programme design, and partnership agreements. Compliance with this policy is a condition of engagement with MRT, and breaches are addressed through the disciplinary and reporting mechanisms described below. MRT reviews this policy at least every two years, or sooner where lessons from a case, an audit, or a change in law require it, to ensure it remains fit for purpose across the contexts in which MRT operates.

## 2 Scope and Application

This policy applies to all MRT staff, whether employed on permanent, fixed-term, or short-term contracts; to volunteers and interns; to consultants and contractors engaged to perform work on MRT's behalf; to board and governance members; and to implementing and downstream partners operating under an MRT partnership or sub-grant agreement. It applies at MRT's headquarters, in every country office, and in every location where MRT-funded activity takes place, including during travel, at project sites, and at any event or gathering connected to MRT's work. The policy governs conduct toward programme participants, community members, colleagues, and any other person an individual encounters by virtue of their association with MRT, whether that contact occurs in person, online, or through MRT-provided communication channels. Partner organisations are required to demonstrate an equivalent standard of safeguarding protection as a condition of funding, and MRT reserves the right to review a partner's safeguarding arrangements at any time. Where a partner's own policy is silent or weaker than this one, this policy applies as the operative standard for anyone delivering MRT-funded work. This scope is deliberately broad: safeguarding is not confined to a single department or a single type of contract, and every person connected to MRT shares responsibility for upholding it.

## 3 Definitions

For the purposes of this policy: "Safeguarding" means the responsibility MRT holds to ensure its staff, operations, and programmes do not harm the people they are designed to help, and that any concerns about harm are dealt with promptly and effectively. "Sexual exploitation" means any actual or attempted abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, or trust for sexual purposes, including profiting monetarily, socially, or politically from the sexual exploitation of another. "Sexual abuse" means the actual or threatened physical intrusion of a sexual nature, whether by force or under unequal or coercive conditions. "Harassment" means unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic, or of a sexual nature, that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating, or offensive environment. "Child" means any person under the age of eighteen, regardless of local age-of-majority law. "Beneficiary" or "programme participant" means any individual who receives assistance, services, or support through an MRT programme. "Safeguarding Focal Point" means the designated individual in each country office responsible for receiving and triaging safeguarding concerns. These definitions apply uniformly across every MRT office and partner engagement, irrespective of local terminology.

## 4 Prohibited Conduct

MRT prohibits all forms of sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, and harassment by anyone connected with the organisation, without exception. Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to: any sexual activity with a child regardless of local age of consent; exchanging money, employment, goods, or services for sexual favours; any sexual relationship involving an abuse of power or trust, including with a programme participant; physical, verbal, or online harassment or bullying; and any act of retaliation against a person who reports a concern in good faith. Prohibited conduct also extends to conduct occurring outside working hours or off MRT premises where it involves a person connected to MRT's work or affects MRT's ability to deliver its mission safely. All staff, volunteers, contractors, and partner personnel who become aware of an actual, suspected, or attempted breach of this policy carry an individual and non-delegable duty to report it promptly through the channels set out in Section 6. Failure to report a known or suspected violation is itself treated as a breach of this policy and may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of engagement and referral to law enforcement where the conduct may constitute a criminal offence.

## 5 Duty to Report

Every person covered by this policy has an affirmative duty to report any concern, suspicion, or disclosure of a safeguarding violation as soon as they become aware of it. Reports should be made through the channels described in Section 6 and must never be investigated informally or independently by the person who receives them. MRT does not require certainty before a report is made — a genuine, good-faith concern is sufficient, and MRT would rather receive and screen out a mistaken report than lose one that turns out to be substantiated. Retaliation of any kind against a person who makes a report in good faith is itself a disciplinary offence, and MRT commits to protecting the confidentiality of anyone who raises a concern to the fullest extent consistent with a fair and effective investigation. Reports involving children are subject to mandatory referral to statutory child protection authorities where local law requires it. Managers and Safeguarding Focal Points who receive a report are personally responsible for escalating it within twenty-four hours and may not decide unilaterally that a report does not warrant escalation. This duty applies equally to concerns about MRT's own staff and to concerns about partner or contractor personnel.
