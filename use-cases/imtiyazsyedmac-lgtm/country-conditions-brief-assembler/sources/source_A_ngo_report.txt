SOURCE A — NGO REPORT
Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion
"State Response to Threats Against Labor Organizers: Annual Report 2025"
Published March 2025. All content in this document is fictional test data,
fabricated for the purpose of testing a document-assembly tool. Salvarion is
not a real country; Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion is not a real
organization. No real person, organization, or government is described or
implicated by this document.

[1] Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion is a nongovernmental organization that
monitors labor rights and state accountability in the Republic of Salvarion.
This report covers incidents reported to our office between January and
December 2024.

[2] Our intake log recorded 61 distinct reports of threats, intimidation, or
physical harm directed at individuals identified by the reporter as current
or former labor union organizers. Of these, 40 reports indicated the
complainant had filed a report with municipal police and received no
documented follow-up within 90 days.

[3] In the case of the Rosemont Textile Workers' Union office attack of
14 June 2024, three individuals affiliated with the union's organizing
committee were assaulted by unidentified men outside the union hall. A police
report was filed the same day. As of the publication date of this report, no
suspect has been identified and no arrest has been made.

[4] Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion's caseworkers documented a consistent
pattern across the reports reviewed: complainants who identified themselves
as union organizers described municipal police as declining to open an
investigation, or opening an investigation that was not meaningfully pursued.
We found no case in our 2024 caseload in which a threat against a labor
organizer resulted in an identified suspect being charged.

[5] We are not aware of any dedicated state unit tasked with investigating
threats against labor organizers as a class during the period covered by this
report. Municipal police handled these reports, when they were handled at
all, through ordinary criminal-complaint channels alongside unrelated cases.

[6] It is our assessment, based on the pattern documented above, that a
person publicly identified as a labor union organizer in Salvarion during
2024 could not reliably expect the state to investigate or prevent threats
made against them on that basis.
