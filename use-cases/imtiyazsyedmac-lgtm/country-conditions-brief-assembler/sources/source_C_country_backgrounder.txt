[1] Labor relations. Salvarion's labor movement has grown more active since
2022, with union membership rising across the textile and agricultural
sectors. This growth has been accompanied by episodic reports of violence
and intimidation directed at organizers, concentrated in the Rosemont and
Calderas regions.

[2] In response to rising reports of intimidation, the Ministry of Labor
announced in September 2024 the formation of a Labor Rights Protection Unit
within the national police, tasked specifically with receiving and
investigating complaints of threats or violence against labor organizers.
The unit's stated mandate includes a 30-day response requirement for
complaints referred to it.

[3] As of this backgrounder's publication date, the Labor Rights Protection
Unit has been formally announced and its leadership appointed. Public
reporting on the unit's caseload, staffing levels in municipal offices
outside the capital, and case-resolution rate is not yet available.

[4] Independent monitors have raised questions about whether the unit's
reach extends to municipal-level complaints filed before its September 2024
formation, and whether smaller municipalities such as Rosemont have
received dedicated unit staff or continue to route complaints through
ordinary police channels.

[5] Salvarion's national government has publicly stated its commitment to
protecting freedom of association and has not, as a matter of official
policy, restricted union organizing activity.
