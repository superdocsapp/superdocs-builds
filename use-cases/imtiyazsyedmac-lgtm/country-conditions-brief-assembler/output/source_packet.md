# Indexed Source Packet


## SRC-A — Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion, “State Response to Threats Against Labor Organizers: Annual Report 2025” (March 2025)

**¶1.** Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion is a nongovernmental organization that monitors labor rights and state accountability in the Republic of Salvarion. This report covers incidents reported to our office between January and December 2024.

**¶2.** Our intake log recorded 61 distinct reports of threats, intimidation, or physical harm directed at individuals identified by the reporter as current or former labor union organizers. Of these, 40 reports indicated the complainant had filed a report with municipal police and received no documented follow-up within 90 days.

**¶3.** In the case of the Rosemont Textile Workers' Union office attack of 14 June 2024, three individuals affiliated with the union's organizing committee were assaulted by unidentified men outside the union hall. A police report was filed the same day. As of the publication date of this report, no suspect has been identified and no arrest has been made.

**¶4.** Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion's caseworkers documented a consistent pattern across the reports reviewed: complainants who identified themselves as union organizers described municipal police as declining to open an investigation, or opening an investigation that was not meaningfully pursued. We found no case in our 2024 caseload in which a threat against a labor organizer resulted in an identified suspect being charged.

**¶5.** We are not aware of any dedicated state unit tasked with investigating threats against labor organizers as a class during the period covered by this report. Municipal police handled these reports, when they were handled at all, through ordinary criminal-complaint channels alongside unrelated cases.

**¶6.** It is our assessment, based on the pattern documented above, that a person publicly identified as a labor union organizer in Salvarion during 2024 could not reliably expect the state to investigate or prevent threats made against them on that basis.


## SRC-B — Salvarion Daily Post, “Union Hall Attacked in Rosemont, Organizers Say Police Slow to Act” (16 June 2024)

**¶1.** Three organizers with the Rosemont Textile Workers' Union were injured Friday evening when a group of unidentified men attacked the union's local office, according to union officials and a police report reviewed by this newspaper.

**¶2.** “We called the police as soon as it happened. An officer came, took a statement, and that was the last we heard,” said union organizing committee member Renata Oskar, one of the three injured. Oskar said she has not been contacted by police since filing her statement on the night of the attack.

**¶3.** A spokesperson for the Rosemont municipal police department confirmed a report was filed but said the department could not comment on an active investigation.

**¶4.** The attack follows a string of similar incidents reported by labor organizers across Salvarion over the past year, according to figures cited by Civil Liberties Watch Salvarion, a labor-rights monitoring group. The group's most recent tally counted dozens of reported threats against union organizers, the large majority of which the group says were not meaningfully investigated by police.

**¶5.** Union leadership has called on the national government to create a dedicated unit for investigating threats against organizers, a step they say has not yet been taken.

**¶6.** The national Interior Ministry did not respond to a request for comment by the time of publication.


## SRC-C — Meridian Country Research Group, “Salvarion Country Conditions Backgrounder, 2025 Edition” (2025)

**¶1.** Labor relations. Salvarion's labor movement has grown more active since 2022, with union membership rising across the textile and agricultural sectors. This growth has been accompanied by episodic reports of violence and intimidation directed at organizers, concentrated in the Rosemont and Calderas regions.

**¶2.** In response to rising reports of intimidation, the Ministry of Labor announced in September 2024 the formation of a Labor Rights Protection Unit within the national police, tasked specifically with receiving and investigating complaints of threats or violence against labor organizers. The unit's stated mandate includes a 30-day response requirement for complaints referred to it.

**¶3.** As of this backgrounder's publication date, the Labor Rights Protection Unit has been formally announced and its leadership appointed. Public reporting on the unit's caseload, staffing levels in municipal offices outside the capital, and case-resolution rate is not yet available.

**¶4.** Independent monitors have raised questions about whether the unit's reach extends to municipal-level complaints filed before its September 2024 formation, and whether smaller municipalities such as Rosemont have received dedicated unit staff or continue to route complaints through ordinary police channels.

**¶5.** Salvarion's national government has publicly stated its commitment to protecting freedom of association and has not, as a matter of official policy, restricted union organizing activity.


## SRC-D — Dr. Elena Voss, Dept. of Comparative Politics, Fictional University of Meridian, “Declaration of Dr. Elena Voss” (2025)

**¶1.** I hold a doctorate in comparative politics and have studied labor movements and state security responses in Salvarion for approximately twelve years. I have not visited Rosemont specifically but have reviewed reporting on the region as part of my ongoing research.

**¶2.** In my professional opinion, labor organizers in Salvarion who are publicly identified as such -- for example, by holding an office in a union's organizing committee, or by being named in press coverage of union activity -- form a socially distinct and recognizable group within Salvarion. Individuals outside the labor movement in Salvarion routinely identify “union organizers” as a distinct category of person, both in ordinary conversation and in how local media and police describe complainants.

**¶3.** It is my opinion that the September 2024 announcement of a Labor Rights Protection Unit represents a genuine policy commitment at the national level, but that as of early 2025 there is not yet public evidence the unit has operational capacity in municipalities outside the capital. I have not seen documentation of the unit taking any specific case referred to it, in Rosemont or elsewhere, to resolution.

**¶4.** Based on the pattern described in independent reporting -- including the Rosemont union hall attack of June 2024, which by all accounts I have reviewed remains without an identified suspect -- it is my assessment that, at the municipal level in Rosemont specifically, a labor organizer facing a threat in the period surrounding mid-2024 would most likely have had their complaint handled through ordinary police channels rather than by the newly announced unit, given the timing and the unit's apparent lack of municipal-level staffing as of this declaration.

**¶5.** I make this declaration based on my professional research and am prepared to testify consistently with it if called upon to do so.
