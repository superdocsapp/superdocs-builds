Nebula Drift - Game Design Document

Vantage Nine Studios - internal - the spine. Numbers live in Mechanics; do not duplicate them here except as prose.

Overview

Nebula Drift is a single-player momentum-platformer set in the wreckage of a collapsed orbital shipyard. The player is a salvager who cannot fight most of what lives there and must out-move it instead.

Design pillars

  • Momentum is the reward. Every system either preserves speed or explains clearly why it took it away.
  • The wreck remembers. Sections the player has stripped stay stripped.
  • Legibility over spectacle. If a hazard cannot be read in one frame at speed, it is cut.

Core loop

  1. Enter a sector and read its silhouette for salvage.
  2. Route through it without stopping.
  3. Strip what you reached; lose what you did not.
  4. Spend salvage on traversal upgrades, never on combat.

Traversal

Traversal is the game. The player has a dash, a wall-run and a grapple. The grapple is the only tool that converts height into speed, so it carries the longest cooldown in the game at 8 s, and every level is paced around that number.

Full parameters live in the Mechanics sub-document, section "Grapple".

Combat

Combat exists to make traversal mistakes expensive. The player has one defensive tool - an overheat pulse - and no weapons.

Progression

Salvage buys traversal upgrades in a flat, non-branching order. No skill tree: the team has shipped one before and it cost six weeks of tuning for no measured retention gain.

Art direction

Readable-industrial. Detail lives in silhouette, not surface. Full rules, palette and reference boards live in the Art Bible sub-document.

Narrative

Told through the wreck itself and one voice on a dying channel. Beat sheet and characters live in the Narrative sub-document.
