# SuperDocs — use cases and who would buy

**Status:** I have not contacted any person or company. This is a research list, not outreach.

**Rule followed:** I applied to both engineer and growth tracks, so this list uses **companies and roles only**. No named people. No emails. No phone numbers.

**Who I already know at these companies:** nobody. That is the normal answer.

**Assigned build this list sits on:** Dropbox folder watcher with write-back (human gate, sibling file). The first use case is the one that build already demos.

---

## 1. Boutique architecture / interior studios (Dropbox as the OS)

A client drops a messy brief into a shared Dropbox inbox. SuperDocs proposes a studio-template proposal or a one-page companion brief. A principal gates it. The export lands beside the original.

- **Buyer role:** studio principal, ops lead, or project coordinator (8–40 people). Not a CIO.
- **Companies that would actually buy:** independent architecture / interior practices that already run the job in Dropbox (local 8–20 person studios; WeWork-style freelance clusters). Regional studios of large firms that still share client folders rather than a full DMS — e.g. a Gensler *regional* studio, not Gensler global IT.
- **Where they listen:** AIA / RIBA chapter talks, Instagram and LinkedIn of peer principals, Dropbox “creative studio” case studies, local architecture Slack/Discord.
- **What opens the door:** a 20-minute screen share of *their* Dropbox inbox: drop a fake brief, show review, show the sibling `.superdocs.` file. Do not start with “AI platform.” Start with “your intern will not retype this brief.”

## 2. Vendor contract + amendment piles (SaaS procurement legal)

MSA plus three amendments. Legal ops needs one register of payment terms, liability caps, and renewal dates, cited to clause and file. Disagreements are conflicts, not silent merges.

- **Buyer role:** head of legal ops or procurement counsel at mid-market SaaS. Not the GC of a mega-cap.
- **Companies:** Freshworks, Zoho, Postman (India / global mid-market legal ops that already live in shared drives).
- **Where they listen:** CLOC, ACC India, Legal Ops Slack communities, “how we run renewals” LinkedIn posts from legal ops leads.
- **What opens the door:** one messy amendment pack vs the original MSA. Ask: “show me the cap that actually governs after amendment 2.” SuperDocs returns a cited conflict. A lawyer still clicks approve.

## 3. Clinical protocol vs lab manual (CRO medical writing)

A protocol amendment must not silently disagree with the lab manual. SuperDocs flags the mismatch with a locator. A person gates the finding before it ships.

- **Buyer role:** medical writing lead or QA of documents at a CRO. Not hospital clinical IT.
- **Companies:** IQVIA, Syneos, PPD (medical writing / document QA groups, not the EHR team).
- **Where they listen:** DIA, medical writing society meetings, LinkedIn of medical writers, internal “document QC” forums.
- **What opens the door:** two synthetic PDFs that disagree on a dose or visit window. Honest “here is the conflict, here is the page.” **Do not claim certification.** Certification is a later procurement conversation.

## 4. Commercial lease abstraction

A 100-page lease becomes a 2-page abstract (rent, break, CAM). An amendment that contradicts the original is a conflict, not an overwrite.

- **Buyer role:** asset manager or lease-admin lead at a real-estate services firm.
- **Companies:** CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield (lease-admin / asset-management desks that already abstract in Word and Dropbox, not the ones locked into a full lease-admin SaaS).
- **Where they listen:** ULI, Bisnow, in-house “lease admin” LinkedIn, PropTech breakfasts.
- **What opens the door:** “your abstract is six weeks behind the last amendment.” Show one amendment that changes the break clause and a human gate before anything is filed.

## 5. Insurance claim pack vs governing policy

Claim file vs the policy: covered / not covered with page pins. A human reviews before anything is sent to a claimant.

- **Buyer role:** TPA claims examiner lead or claims-quality manager.
- **Companies:** Sedgwick, Gallagher Bassett (examiner / quality teams, not the carrier’s core policy admin system).
- **Where they listen:** PLRB, claims-association webinars, LinkedIn of TPA quality managers.
- **What opens the door:** a synthetic claim letter that quotes the wrong exclusion. SuperDocs pins the policy page. The examiner still decides.

## 6. Construction weekly pack

Site notes + invoices + change orders → one status brief. A change order that breaks the baseline is surfaced.

- **Buyer role:** PMO / project controls lead at a general contractor.
- **Companies:** Turner, Skanska, L&T Construction (project-controls desks that already dump weekly packs into shared folders).
- **Where they listen:** AGC, Autodesk Construction events, project-controls LinkedIn, “look-ahead schedule” Slack of PMs.
- **What opens the door:** last week’s change-order PDF vs the baseline. “This CO blows the allowance — cited.” A PM still approves the brief.

## 7. Bank internal manual vs new circular

A SEBI / RBI / FINRA-style bulletin vs the internal procedure: which paragraphs aged out.

- **Buyer role:** compliance change-management analyst (not the CISO, not retail branch ops).
- **Companies:** HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, a US broker-dealer compliance change team that still diffs circulars in Word.
- **Where they listen:** IBA / NISM events, compliance officer LinkedIn, circular-digest newsletters.
- **What opens the door:** one public circular vs a synthetic internal SOP. “These three clauses are stale.” A compliance officer gates before the SOP is republished. **Do not claim we replace the GRC system.**

## 8. RFP response factory

A 200-page RFP becomes a checklist. Answers are drafted from prior winning text. A bid lead still reviews.

- **Buyer role:** bid / proposal manager at an IT services firm.
- **Companies:** Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, IBM India bid desks (proposal ops, not corporate strategy).
- **Where they listen:** APMP, bid-manager LinkedIn, Shipley-style workshops.
- **What opens the door:** “section 4.2 of this RFP vs last year’s winning annex.” Draft + citations + human gate. SuperDocs is not the bid CRM.

## 9. Diligence folders (law firm / PE ops) — SuperDocs is not the VDR

Employment agreements land in a shared folder. Change-of-control clauses become findings. The product is the finding, not the archive.

- **Buyer role:** associate or PE ops analyst who already reviews packs; **not** the records manager buying a DMS.
- **Companies:** Khaitan & Co, AZB & Partners, a PE ops team at a house like KKR (deal ops, not Iron Mountain).
- **Where they listen:** IBA, PE ops roundtables, associate WhatsApp groups that we **do not join or message**. Public: legal-tech panels.
- **What opens the door:** one synthetic SPA vs the employment agreement. “Change-of-control is here, cited.” They still own privilege and the VDR.

## Poor fits (shown with evidence — worth as much as a tidy row)

### P1. Native e-sign as the product (DocuSign / Adobe Sign replacement)

**Finding:** poor first market for SuperDocs.

**Evidence:** the assigned SuperDocs contract is upload → chat → human approve → export. That is gated editing, not a legally binding signature workflow. DocuSign and Adobe Sign already own envelope, certificate, and audit-trail procurement. Selling “we replace DocuSign” fights a category SuperDocs is not in. **Door we would not open:** a VP of Sales Ops whose KPI is time-to-signature.

### P2. Hospital EHR / in-ward clinical systems as buyer #1

**Finding:** poor first market.

**Evidence:** hospitals buy through Epic / Oracle Health (Cerner) app-orchards, HITRUST / HIPAA BAAs, and 12–18 month IT security reviews. SuperDocs has no certification claim in this round. A medical-writing desk at a CRO (use case 3) is a real buyer *role*; a hospital EHR committee is not. **Door we would not open:** a hospital CIO RFP.

### P3. Replacing SharePoint / iManage / a DMS

**Finding:** poor fit if the pitch is “we are the system of record.”

**Evidence:** SuperDocs does not store the firm’s corpus as the archive. Diligence (use case 9) only works if we sit *beside* Dropbox or a VDR. Pitching against iManage or SharePoint is a lost enterprise deal. **Door we would not open:** a records manager whose job is retention and hold.

3. **Do not lead with banks, hospitals, or e-sign.** Those procurement cycles bury a seed product.
