# Developer Advocate

## Team
Developer Relations

## Role purpose
Make Northstar's public APIs and sample apps visible to external developers through talks, writing, community presence, and conference work.

## Responsibilities
- Write tutorials, sample applications, and blog posts aimed at developers who do not work at Northstar.
- Speak at conferences and meetups; host livestreams that walk through public API use.
- Maintain the public Discord and GitHub discussions, including answering questions outside business hours when a thread is moving.
- Collect anecdotal sentiment from the community and share stories internally; you are not the owner of the product backlog.
- Produce demo booth content and swag copy for events.
- Grow newsletter and social audience metrics agreed with marketing.
- Travel frequently; success is measured by reach, content output, and community engagement, not by service uptime or revenue.

## Scope and autonomy
You own the external developer-community program. You choose topics, events, and content formats within a marketing budget. You do not own a production service, a product surface, or a customer account. You cannot commit engineering time or roadmap items. Internal teams may ignore your sentiment reports.

## Leadership and management
No direct reports. Community moderators are volunteers. You do not hire engineers or run customer success.

## Stakeholders
- External developers and community moderators
- Marketing, for brand and event spend
- Occasional product engineers who review a sample app for accuracy
- Conference organizers
- The head of Developer Relations for budget and travel approval

## Requirements
- Public speaking and technical writing aimed at practitioners.
- Ability to build small sample apps that compile and look credible on stage.
- Comfort with social and community tools.
- Willingness to travel and to work event-driven hours.
- Enthusiasm for Northstar's public platform as a subject, not as an internal service to operate.
