# Cost variance summary for active client engagements

## Project performance overview

We examined budget data for two client engagements ending on 13 August 2026. Project Alpha incurred total costs of USD 120,500 against a baseline of USD 100,000. Project Beta spent USD 85,000 against a budget of USD 90,000. These figures show a clear split in our financial execution—one account exceeded projections while the other remained under budget. 

Unanticipated scope additions drove the negative variance on Project Alpha. The client requested three extra workshops that required senior staff participation. Billable hours exceeded our initial estimates by 20 percent. We absorbed some expenses internally to protect the relationship.

## Comparative analysis

Project Beta achieved a positive cost variance through strict scope control. The project manager tracked all deliverables against the original statement of work. We completed the engagement 2 weeks ahead of schedule.

Internal data confirms that scope creep remains our primary profit risk (Smith, 2024). We must apply these lessons to upcoming proposals. Consider the following actions for future accounts:

- Enforce strict change order protocols
- Review milestone allocations every 14 days
- Cap unbilled advisory hours at 5 percent
