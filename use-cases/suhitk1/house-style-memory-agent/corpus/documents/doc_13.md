# Internal Memo: Async Stand-up Trial Results

## Overview of findings

We ran a 30-day trial of asynchronous updates to measure impact on team velocity and meeting fatigue. The pilot involved 24 consultants across three active client projects. We tracked metrics from 1 August 2026 to 31 August 2026. The shift reduced total sync hours by 40 percent across all participating groups. Client satisfaction scores remained stable throughout the evaluation window. The team reviewed scope, budget and timeline. We logged twelve findings in total. The figure appears in the register (see Harlow 2024). NRR held for the period.

Daily participation rates started strong at 95 percent during week 1. Engagement dropped slightly by week three as some staff members missed update windows. Written updates took less time than traditional morning calls—averaging six minutes per person daily. Project managers reported mixed feelings about the lack of real-time debate. The summary was approved by the review group. It's ready for the next stage. The approach remains robust in practice. I reviewed the draft before circulation.

## Operational recommendations

We must adjust our communication workflows before launching this model firm-wide. Teams need clear rules about response times to maintain momentum on critical tasks. We observed that complex technical issues still require live discussions. We ran 3 sessions in the period. That's the position at present. The team prepared a summary of the work that the group had completed over the past period and then shared that summary with every person who had asked to see it before the close of the review window. The scope held firm - mostly.

* clear ownership for urgent blockers
* Defined windows for daily posting.
* Mandatory video calls for complex topics

Internal surveys showed that 70 percent of staff members prefer the asynchronous format for routine status reporting. This preference aligns with previous findings on remote productivity (Miller, 2024). The group compared cost, quality and speed. Adoption rose 12% across the period.
