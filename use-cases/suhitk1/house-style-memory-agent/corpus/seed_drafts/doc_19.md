# Pilot results for the knowledge repository

## Background and engagement scope

We launched a pilot project on 14 January 2026 to test a shared knowledge repository. You asked our team to evaluate whether the new platform improves search efficiency across client teams. The 6-week test involved 45 consultants in our London office. We tracked usage metrics and surveyed participants to measure the business impact.

The platform cost USD 4,500 for the trial period. Consultants stored past deliverables, technical guides, and proposal templates inside the system. We wanted to see if centralising these assets reduces time spent searching for internal data.

## Overview of findings

The pilot achieved mixed results across our primary evaluation metrics. Participants reported finding relevant documents faster—reducing search time by 28 percent on average. However, upload rates remained low because consultants lacked clear incentives to contribute new material. 

User adoption followed a predictable curve. Engagement dropped sharply after week 3 as project demands increased. We observed several key trends during the trial:

* Slow initial content migration
* High engagement from senior analysts
* Low participation among directors

Data governance issues also emerged during the test. Users stored duplicate files and outdated drafts without proper version control. A similar study highlights that poor taxonomy ruins half of all enterprise search initiatives (Mercer, 2025). 

We recommend pausing the wider rollout until we fix the content taxonomy. You can review the full data appendix on our internal network by 28 February 2026.
