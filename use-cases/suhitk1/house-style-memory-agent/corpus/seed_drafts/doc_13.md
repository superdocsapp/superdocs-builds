# Internal memo: async stand-up trial results

## Overview of findings

We ran a 30-day trial of asynchronous updates to measure impact on team velocity and meeting fatigue. The pilot involved 24 consultants across 3 active client projects. We tracked metrics from 1 August 2026 to 31 August 2026. The shift reduced total sync hours by 40 percent across all participating groups. Client satisfaction scores remained stable throughout the evaluation window. 

Daily participation rates started strong at 95 percent during week 1. Engagement dropped slightly by week 3 as some staff members missed update windows. Written updates took less time than traditional morning calls—averaging 6 minutes per person daily. Project managers reported mixed feelings about the lack of real-time debate. 

## Operational recommendations

We must adjust our communication workflows before launching this model firm-wide. Teams need clear rules about response times to maintain momentum on critical tasks. We observed that complex technical issues still require live discussions. 

* Clear ownership for urgent blockers
* Defined windows for daily posting
* Mandatory video calls for complex topics

Internal surveys showed that 70 percent of staff members prefer the asynchronous format for routine status reporting. This preference aligns with previous findings on remote productivity (Miller, 2024). 

Please review these findings before our leadership meeting on 15 September 2026. We will decide on firm-wide adoption during that session.
