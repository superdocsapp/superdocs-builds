## Abstract

Mitochondrial dysfunction is increasingly recognised as a central mechanism in neurodegenerative disease. In this study, we investigated the role of dynamin-related protein 1 (DRP1) in mediating mitochondrial fragmentation in a cellular model of Parkinson's disease. We treated SH-SY5Y cells with 1-methyl-4-phenylpyridinium (MPP+) at concentrations of 0.5 mM, 1.0 mM, and 2.0 mM for 24 hours. Mitochondrial morphology was quantified using confocal microscopy and automated image analysis. We found that MPP+ treatment induced a dose-dependent increase in mitochondrial fragmentation (p < 0.001, n = 45 per group). DRP1 inhibition with Mdivi-1 (50 μM) significantly attenuated fragmentation (p = 0.003) and improved cell viability by 34% ± 6% compared to MPP+-treated controls. These findings suggest that DRP1-mediated fission is a tractable therapeutic target in Parkinson's disease models.

## Introduction

Parkinson's disease (PD) affects approximately 1% of the population over 60 years of age, with an estimated 10 million people affected worldwide [1]. The pathological hallmarks of PD include the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and the accumulation of α-synuclein in Lewy bodies [2,3].

Mitochondrial dysfunction has emerged as a key contributor to PD pathogenesis. Mutations in PINK1 and Parkin, which regulate mitochondrial quality control, cause familial forms of PD [4]. Furthermore, complex I inhibition by environmental toxins such as rotenone and MPP+ recapitulates many features of PD in cellular and animal models [5].

DRP1 is a GTPase that mediates mitochondrial fission by oligomerising at the outer mitochondrial membrane [6]. Excessive DRP1-mediated fission leads to mitochondrial fragmentation, impaired bioenergetics, and increased susceptibility to apoptosis [7]. In our previous work, we demonstrated that DRP1 phosphorylation at Ser616 is elevated in post-mortem PD brain tissue (Smith et al., 2019) [8]. Building on these findings, the present study examines whether pharmacological inhibition of DRP1 can protect against MPP+-induced mitochondrial fragmentation and cell death.

## Methods

### Cell culture

SH-SY5Y human neuroblastoma cells were maintained in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37°C in 5% CO2. Cells were passaged every 3–4 days and used between passages 15 and 25.

### MPP+ treatment

Cells were seeded at 2 × 10^5 cells/well in 6-well plates and allowed to adhere for 24 hours. MPP+ (Sigma-Aldrich, M0896) was dissolved in sterile water and added to the culture medium at final concentrations of 0.5 mM, 1.0 mM, and 2.0 mM. Vehicle-treated controls received an equivalent volume of sterile water. Cells were incubated for 24 hours prior to analysis.

### DRP1 inhibition

Mdivi-1 (Sigma-Aldrich, M0199) was dissolved in DMSO at a stock concentration of 10 mM and diluted to a working concentration of 50 μM in culture medium. Cells were pre-treated with Mdivi-1 for 1 hour prior to MPP+ addition.

### Mitochondrial morphology analysis

Cells were stained with MitoTracker Red CMXRos (100 nM, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 30 minutes at 37°C. Confocal images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM 880 microscope with a 63× oil-immersion objective (NA 1.4). Mitochondrial morphology was quantified using the MiNA ImageJ macro [9]. The fragmentation index was calculated as the ratio of individual mitochondria to total mitochondrial objects per cell. A minimum of 30 cells per condition were analysed across three independent experiments (n = 45 total per group after exclusion of cells with poor staining quality).

### Cell viability

Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay (Sigma-Aldrich, M5655) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a FLUOstar Omega plate reader (BMG Labtech). Results are expressed as percentage viability relative to vehicle-treated controls.

### Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) unless otherwise stated. Statistical comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism 9.0.

## Results

### MPP+ induces dose-dependent mitochondrial fragmentation

Treatment with MPP+ induced a dose-dependent increase in the mitochondrial fragmentation index compared to vehicle-treated controls (Figure 1A). At 0.5 mM MPP+, the fragmentation index increased by 1.8-fold (p = 0.012). At 1.0 mM, the increase was 2.9-fold (p < 0.001), and at 2.0 mM, the increase was 4.1-fold (p < 0.001). One-way ANOVA confirmed a significant effect of MPP+ concentration (F(3,176) = 47.3, p < 0.001).

### DRP1 inhibition attenuates fragmentation

Pre-treatment with Mdivi-1 (50 μM) significantly reduced MPP+-induced fragmentation at all concentrations tested (Figure 1B). In cells treated with 1.0 mM MPP+, Mdivi-1 reduced the fragmentation index from 2.9-fold to 1.4-fold above control (p = 0.003, t(88) = 3.1). The effect of Mdivi-1 was not statistically significant in vehicle-treated cells (p = 0.41), indicating that the drug does not affect baseline mitochondrial morphology.

### DRP1 inhibition improves cell viability

MPP+ treatment at 1.0 mM reduced cell viability to 58% ± 8% of control (p < 0.001). Mdivi-1 pre-treatment improved viability to 77% ± 7% of control, representing a 34% ± 6% improvement relative to MPP+-treated cells (p = 0.002, n = 45 per group). At 2.0 mM MPP+, Mdivi-1 did not significantly rescue viability (p = 0.18), suggesting that at high toxin concentrations, DRP1-independent mechanisms contribute to cell death.

---

## Discussion

Our findings demonstrate that DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission is a significant contributor to MPP+-induced neuronal cell death in SH-SY5Y cells. The dose-dependent relationship between MPP+ concentration and mitochondrial fragmentation is consistent with previous reports [5,10]. The ability of Mdivi-1 to attenuate fragmentation and improve viability at moderate MPP+ concentrations supports the hypothesis that DRP1 inhibition may be neuroprotective.

The incomplete rescue of viability at 2.0 mM MPP+ suggests that at high toxin concentrations, additional cell death pathways are engaged that are independent of DRP1-mediated fission. This is consistent with reports of MPP+-induced oxidative stress and direct complex I inhibition [11].

A limitation of this study is the use of SH-SY5Y cells, which are a transformed cell line and may not fully recapitulate the biology of primary dopaminergic neurons. Future studies should validate these findings in primary neuronal cultures and in vivo models.

In conclusion, our data support DRP1 as a tractable therapeutic target in cellular models of PD. Pharmacological inhibition of DRP1 with Mdivi-1 attenuates mitochondrial fragmentation and improves cell viability under conditions of moderate mitochondrial stress.
