# A Ledger in Three Hands

The Northern Company's field ledger for 1971 is written in three hands, and the third
one is the problem.

The first hand belongs to Barrow, small and upright. The second belongs to a surveyor
identified in the pay records only as "J.M.", who wrote in pencil and pressed hard.
The third hand appears only between August and October, always in ink, always filling
in totals at the foot of a page, and never signing anything.

![[Figures/farm-gate-detail.png|The farm gate at Cowley Bottom, from which the phantom footpath was drawn north.]]

What the third hand did was reconcile. Where two surveyors had walked overlapping
ground and returned different measurements, someone in the office chose a number. The
ledger records the chosen number and not the two it replaced.[^reconcile]

[^reconcile]: Ndiaye and colleagues call this "silent reconciliation" and argue it is
    endemic to survey work under deadline [@ndiaye2015]. Their estimate is that
    between four and nine per cent of published measurements in the period were
    reconciled rather than observed.

> [!note] A note on the archive
> The ledger is held at Fenwick & Hale and may be consulted by appointment. Pages 88
> to 94 are missing and have been missing since at least 1990.

The footpath at Cowley Bottom — the one that sent me into this — first appears as a
totalled figure in the third hand, on a page where no surveyor's initials appear at
all. Nobody walked it. Somebody added it up.

## Reading the reconciliation

You can find the reconciled entries by looking for arithmetic that is too clean. A
column of nine field measurements, each taken with a chain over uneven ground, does
not sum to a round number. When it does, someone has decided.
