# The Blank Interior

Before a map can be wrong it has to be finished, and the pressure to finish is older
than the pressure to be right.

The Northern Survey of 1969 to 1973 was commissioned with a fixed budget and a fixed
end date, in that order. Nine surveyors were sent out with instructions that ran to
four pages, of which three concerned the return of equipment. The fourth said that
where ground truth could not be established, the previous edition was to be carried
forward.

![[Figures/north-sheet-4.png|Sheet 4 of the Northern Survey, showing the interior left substantially as drawn in 1938.]]

That single sentence is the origin of most of what this book is about. Carrying the
previous edition forward is not laziness; it is the only defensible thing to do when
you have not been somewhere. The failure is not in carrying it forward. The failure is
that the map does not say which parts were carried and which were walked.[^carried]

[^carried]: The 1:25000 series did not distinguish surveyed from carried-forward
    features until the 2011 revision, and then only in the sheet metadata rather than
    on the face of the map.

Ines Barrow, who walked the eastern quarter of the survey area, kept notebooks that
survive in the Fenwick archive [@barrow1971]. Her entry for 14 August 1971 reads, in
full: "Fog. Did not go up. Copied 38."

## What a blank means

A blank on a map is a statement about the mapmaker, not about the ground. There is no
such thing as an area with nothing in it; there are only areas nobody was paid to
visit.^[The convention of leaving genuinely unsurveyed interiors blank, rather than
plausible, is roughly two centuries old and is not universal even now.]

The interior of Sheet 4, shown in [[Figures/north-sheet-4.png]], is not empty ground.
It is 340 square kilometres that the 1971 survey did not enter, drawn from a 1938
edition that had itself carried forward from 1913.
