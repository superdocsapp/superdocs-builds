# The Second Walk

In the spring of 2019 I walked the eastern quarter of the survey area with Barrow's
notebooks in a plastic bag, trying to stand where she had stood.

You cannot, quite. The field boundaries have moved; the wood at Cowley has been felled
and replanted forty metres to the west; the farm gate is a different gate on the same
hinge posts. But the ridge lines do not move, and from the ridge you can see what she
saw, which is a great deal of ground and no obvious way to cross it quickly.

Her entry for the day I was trying to reproduce reads: "Up at 5. Cloud to 400. Walked
the spur to the second cairn and turned back. Nothing new."

Nothing new is the most honest sentence in the archive.

## What I got wrong

I had assumed, for about two years, that the Cowley Bottom footpath was a trap street.
It has the shape of one: a short dead-ended feature, in an area of low traffic, with no
corresponding ground truth.

It is not. The Northern Survey was a public series and did not use traps; Harrow's
account of the committee makes clear that the practice was explicitly rejected in 1969
on the grounds that a public map may not contain a deliberate falsehood
[@harrow2004].

I was wrong for two years because the wrong answer was more interesting than the right
one. The right one is that a tired man in an office added up a column.
