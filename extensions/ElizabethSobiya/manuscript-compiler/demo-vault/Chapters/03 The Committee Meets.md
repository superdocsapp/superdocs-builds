# The Committee Meets

In February 1972 the survey's oversight committee met to consider whether the
coastline of the northern estuary should be redrawn.

The minutes run to eleven pages. Nine of them concern the estuary. Two concern
whether the committee had the authority to consider the estuary at all.

![[Figures/committee-minutes.png|Minutes of the oversight committee, 9 February 1972, page 3.]]

Petra Harrow has written the definitive account of this meeting [@harrow2004], and her
reading is that the committee's real subject was not the coastline but the precedent:
if a published line could be moved on the evidence of one surveyor's disagreement,
every published line was in principle movable, and the series would never close.

They voted six to three to leave it. The estuary as drawn in 1972 is the estuary as
drawn in 1938, and it is roughly 200 metres wrong at its widest.

## The precedent argument, stated fairly

It is easy to read the committee as cowardly. It is worth stating their case as they
would have.

A map series is a promise of consistency. A user who buys Sheet 4 in 1972 and Sheet 5
in 1974 needs the two to agree at the join. Moving a line on one sheet, on evidence
that has not been applied to its neighbours, breaks that promise in a way that is
invisible to the user and therefore worse than the original error.[^join]

[^join]: This is not a hypothetical. The 1963 revision of the western series moved a
    river on one sheet and not its neighbour, and the resulting mismatch at the join
    was not corrected for nineteen years.

The committee was not defending the line. It was defending the join.
